Paris Saint-Germain paid Neymar's €222m release clause in August 2017. The
previous record, Paul Pogba to Manchester United, had been €105m a year earlier.
Records are normally broken by ten or twenty per cent. This one was doubled.

## The mechanism that mattered

Because the fee was a buyout clause rather than a negotiated price, Barcelona
could not refuse it. That detail turned every large release clause in Spain from
a theoretical deterrent into a published price, and clubs have written them
differently ever since.

## What it did to the market

Not much at the top: no fee has come close since, and the very largest transfers
have if anything cooled. The effect was two divisions down. A fee of €60m
stopped being remarkable overnight, and selling clubs recalibrated their opening
positions accordingly. The floor rose even though the ceiling did not.

Kylian Mbappé arrived at PSG in the same window on a loan structured to become a
€180m obligation, which was widely read as an attempt to keep the summer's
spending inside financial fair play. The club were later sanctioned and the
rules were rewritten.

Whether the football was worth it is a separate and much shorter argument.
