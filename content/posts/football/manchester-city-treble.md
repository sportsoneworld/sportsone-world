Manchester City became the second English club, after Manchester United in 1999,
to win the league, the FA Cup and the European Cup in one season. The final was
settled by a Rodri goal against Internazionale, and it was not a comfortable
night.

## The change that made the difference

Midway through the season Pep Guardiola began inverting John Stones — a centre
back stepping into midfield when City had the ball, giving them a numerical
advantage in the middle third without committing another attacker.

The effect was defensive as much as creative. City's problem in previous
European campaigns had been the transition after losing the ball high up the
pitch. An extra body in central midfield shortened the distance the first
defender had to cover, and the counter-attacks that had knocked them out in
previous years stopped arriving.

## What it cost

A more controlled side scored slightly less freely and won more matches by one
goal. Erling Haaland's 36 league goals get the headline, but City's actual
improvement that season was at the other end.

The idea has since spread far beyond Manchester, which is the usual fate of a
Guardiola solution: it wins something, and then it becomes a coaching-badge
module.
