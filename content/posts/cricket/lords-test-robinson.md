England bowled Pakistan out for 110 on the second day of the second Test at
Lord's, a first-innings lead of 180 that leaves them a day and a half to win
the match and the series.

Ollie Robinson took four wickets for eleven runs. Jofra Archer had removed Shan
Masood for one in the third over, leaving Pakistan five for one, and the innings
never recovered from it. The last five wickets went down for 19 runs in seven
overs.

## The collapse

Robinson and Archer bowled unchanged through the closing passage. Pakistan lost
five for 19 in seven overs either side of the interval — a rate of decline that
turned a difficult position into an untenable one before the crowd had settled
back into their seats.

England's four seamers have now bowled Pakistan out cheaply twice in a series
that was one-sided at Headingley and has been one-sided again here.

## How England got there

England were 77 for four on the first morning and looked like conceding the
initiative. Jamie Smith made 61 and Jordan Cox 55, and the pair carried the
innings to 248 for nine by the close. The tail added enough on the second
morning to reach 290.

By the close of the second day England were 81 for three in their second
innings, Emilio Gay unbeaten on 30. The lead is already past 250.

## What it means for the series

Joe Root took the England captaincy full time this summer, having stood in for
the suspended Ben Stokes against New Zealand at the Oval. Stephen Fleming's
arrival as head coach was reported to be part of what changed his mind. A pace
attack capable of two performances like this one will not have hurt.
