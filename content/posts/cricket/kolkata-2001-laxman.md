Australia arrived in Kolkata in March 2001 on a run of sixteen consecutive Test
wins. They bowled India out for 171, made 445 themselves, and enforced the
follow-on. Nothing about the first three days suggested the streak was in
danger.

## The fourth day

VVS Laxman and Rahul Dravid batted through all of it. They put on 376 for the
fifth wicket without being separated once between lunch on day four and the
morning of day five. Laxman made 281, then the highest score by an Indian in
Tests. Dravid made 180 from number six, a position he had not asked for.

## The finish

India declared on 657 for seven and left themselves 75 overs. Harbhajan Singh,
who had taken a hat-trick in the first innings, took six more in the second.
Australia lost by 171 runs, having been ahead by 274 on the first innings.

It is one of three occasions in the history of Test cricket that a side has won
after following on. What it settled was less technical than that: for the rest
of that decade, an Australian team that beat everybody else could not reliably
beat India in India.
