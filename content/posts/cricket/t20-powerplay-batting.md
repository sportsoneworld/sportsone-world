For the first six overs of a Twenty20 innings only two fielders may stand
outside the thirty-yard circle. It is the only phase of the game in which the
gaps are guaranteed rather than earned, and for most of the format's history
sides used it to survive.

## The change

Scoring rates in the powerplay have risen across every major league, and the
rise is not evenly distributed. It is concentrated in teams with an opener who
attacks square of the wicket, because that is where the two permitted boundary
riders cannot both be.

## The cost of getting it wrong

An opener who scores at nine an over through the powerplay and gets out in the
seventh is worth more than one who scores at seven and bats to the fifteenth.
That is a genuinely counter-intuitive result and it took the market a decade to
price it.

The trade-off is real: attacking hard with two fielders out means losing
wickets, and a side three down after six overs has a middle order playing a
different, slower game than the one it was picked for. The best teams accept
that and pick a middle order that can rebuild. The rest attack, collapse, and
conclude that the powerplay is dangerous.
