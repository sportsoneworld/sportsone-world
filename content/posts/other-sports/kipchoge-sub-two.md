Eliud Kipchoge covered 42.195 kilometres in 1 hour 59 minutes and 40.2 seconds
in Vienna on 12 October 2019. It was not ratified as a world record and was
never intended to be.

## What made it possible

A rotating team of 41 pacemakers running in a formation designed to reduce drag,
a car projecting a laser line onto the road to set the pace, drinks delivered by
bicycle rather than collected from a table, a flat closed circuit and a start
time chosen for temperature.

Each of those breaks a competition rule. Together they are worth, by most
estimates, somewhere between one and two minutes over the distance.

## What it still demonstrated

That the physiological barrier people had assumed existed did not. Kipchoge's
official world record at the time was 2:01:39, set in Berlin in 2018 under
ordinary conditions; he lowered it to 2:01:09 in 2022. The gap between what a
person can do in a race and what one can do in a laboratory-grade setup is now a
known quantity rather than a mystery.

The honest framing is the one Kipchoge used: an exhibition that proved a point,
not a race that he won.
