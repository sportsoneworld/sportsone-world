Roger Federer had won the previous five Wimbledon titles. Rafael Nadal had lost
the last two finals to him, and had beaten him at Roland Garros a month earlier
without dropping a set.

## The match

Nadal won the first two sets. Federer saved two championship points in the
fourth-set tie-break and took it 10-8. Two rain delays broke the rhythm of the
fifth. Nadal won it 9-7 at 9.15 in the evening, in light poor enough that play
could not have gone much further.

Four hours and forty-eight minutes of play made it the longest Wimbledon final
at the time.

## Why it endures

Partly the quality, which was extremely high in the fourth and fifth sets rather
than throughout. Mostly because it resolved something. Nadal was, until that
evening, a clay-court player who competed elsewhere. He won Wimbledon again the
following year he entered, and the Olympic gold in Beijing weeks later, and went
to number one in August.

Federer won Wimbledon twice more. But the assumption that grass belonged to him
did not survive the summer.
